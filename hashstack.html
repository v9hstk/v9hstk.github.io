Hashstack Finance: Product Design Decisions in Under-Collateralized DeFi Lending

Protocol design · interest rate innovation · strategic wind-down · 2020–2026

Executive Summary

Hashstack Finance was a DeFi lending protocol on Starknet L2 that enabled under-collateralized borrowing — a product category where every other player relied on human credit assessment. Hashstack did it autonomously, using smart contract constraints instead of credit committees. Two product innovations — Degen Mode (one-click leveraged execution) and DIAL (a custom interest rate algorithm) — demonstrated original product thinking in a crowded market. The protocol was sunset in 2026 after Starknet ecosystem adoption lagged growth targets and Cairo 2.0 migration costs exceeded projected ROI.

1. Industry Context: DeFi Lending in 2020

Decentralized lending is one of the largest sectors in DeFi. Users deposit cryptocurrency as collateral, borrow other assets against it, and pay interest — similar to a bank loan, but executed by smart contracts without a bank in the middle.

The dominant protocols — Aave, Compound, and MakerDAO — all require over-collateralization. To borrow $75, you must deposit $100. This protects the protocol but creates a capital efficiency problem: you need more money than you want to borrow.

By 2020, this model had reached $10B+ in total value locked. It worked, but it locked enormous amounts of capital in unproductive collateral positions.

2. The Thesis: Autonomous Under-Collateralized Lending

Several protocols attempted under-collateralized lending before Hashstack. The difference was how risk was managed.

Hashstack’s thesis was that under-collateralized lending could work without human gatekeeping if borrowed capital was constrained to approved deployment channels within the protocol’s integrated ecosystem. The smart contract itself enforced solvency — no credit committee, no whitelist, no institutional-only access.

This is the same collateral-vs-access trade-off that underlies all lending: how much freedom do you give the borrower while protecting the lender? Hashstack’s answer was maximum leverage, minimum withdrawal.

3. Product Evolution: V1 to V2

The protocol shipped two distinct versions, each representing a different risk calibration.

The V1 model was documented in the protocol’s public seed funding announcement: borrow up to $300 by providing only $100 as collateral; of this, withdraw $70 (up to 70% collateral) while utilizing $230 as in-platform trading capital.

The evolution from V1 to V2 increased leverage from 3× to 5× while removing the withdrawal option entirely — more capital to deploy, no escape hatch that created risk without user value. Extra supply above the $1,000 minimum reduced leverage automatically (e.g. $2,500 deposit → 2.5× instead of 5×).

4. Product Innovation I: Degen Mode

The Problem

Manual leveraged yield in DeFi requires choosing pools, calculating ratios, borrowing, swapping, and providing LP — each step with gas, slippage, and error risk. Most users lack the expertise.

The Product Decision

Degen Mode abstracted that sequence into one click: ranked strategies (returns, APR, depth), select, Execute — protocol handles the rest atomically.

How It Worked

  1. Supply $1,000+ in a single asset
  2. Open the Degen tab — strategies with estimated APR and risk
  3. Select a strategy, click Execute
  4. Protocol auto-borrows $5,000 and deploys in one transaction
  5. Track results on Your Borrow

5. Product Innovation II: Interest Rate Design (DIAL → Production Kink)

Aave and Compound use a single floating rate per asset from instantaneous utilization. Hashstack needed commitment periods (2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months) with higher APR for longer lockups — a different rate model.

DIAL: The Ambitious Version

  • Term structure pricing — longer lockups earned higher APR (correlation ladder ~1.2× between tiers).
  • Bounded rates — supply ≤15%, borrow ≤20% to avoid extreme Slope2-style spikes.
  • Anti-manipulation — keccak256-based pseudo-random sampling of update timing (not VRF); proportionate to threat within bounded bands.
  • Multi-tranche accounting — borrower interest ≥ depositor interest + protocol spread.
  • Regime rules — below 25% utilization: supply APR 0%; above 80%: premium on longest-term supply.

What Didn’t Work

  • Non-deterministic rates hurt integrators and risk desks.
  • Admin-triggered updates were slow in a squeeze vs continuous kink models.
  • Multi-tranche + randomization raised audit / formal-verification cost.

The Production Decision

For V1 testnet (July 2023), the model evolved to a kinked utilization curve.

7. The Platform Bet: Starknet

Hashstack migrated to Starknet in August 2022 for ZK-proof cost advantages, Cairo’s formal-verification properties for liquidation logic, and ecosystem support.

The technical thesis validated — the protocol worked on Starknet (utilization and users as operator metrics above; CertiK passed). Ecosystem adoption and tooling lagged EVM alternatives; Cairo hiring was harder than Solidity.

Source: Starknet migration announcement.

8. The Wind-Down Decision

External trigger: Cairo 0 deprecation forced a full Cairo 2.0 migration or exit.

Strategic evaluation: Migration cost was high; projected user growth on Starknet’s trajectory did not justify the investment. A Base testnet showed EVM portability, but the fast-follower window on EVM lending had closed.

Decision: structured wind-down with user fund safety as priority.

9. Key Takeaways

On product design: The constraint is the product. Under-collateralized lending works when you control where capital goes. Moving from 3×/70% withdrawal to 5×/zero withdrawal shows that loosening leverage and tightening containment can happen together.

On technical ambition: Build ambitious to learn what matters, then simplify for production. DIAL explored the design space; the kink curve kept the insights and cut the complexity.

On platform bets: Weight ecosystem go-to-market capacity as heavily as technical fundamentals. A product can be technically sound and still fail if the infrastructure does not reach critical mass.
